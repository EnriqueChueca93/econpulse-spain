How to read it

National accounts. All figures are the IGAE's national accounts of the public administrations (SEC 2010): what was spent and earned in each year, whether paid in cash or not, on the same basis for every administration and every year, so they compare cleanly. They are not the budget the Cortes approves, which is a plan, on a cash-and-commitment basis. The latest year is provisional{% if bx.provisional_note %} ({{ bx.provisional_note }}){% endif %}.

Functions follow the UN's classification of the functions of government (COFOG): 10 divisions, each split into groups. Types group the economic transactions: pay, purchases, social benefits, interest, subsidies, investment and transfers. Spending by function comes a year after spending by type, and the two are published separately, so their totals can differ by a little.

Flows between administrations. Much of what the State spends it passes on: to the regions under their financing system, to local councils, and to the Social Security. In the national accounts these transfers count as spending of the State and as revenue of whoever receives them (COFOG group 01.8, "transfers of a general character between different levels of government"). The switch leaves them out, to show what each administration spends on itself. For all public administrations together the accounts are consolidated, so they cancel out.

The autonomous communities. Their spending by function is published community by community from 2012, by division only; their revenue and spending by type from 2000, and their taxes one by one from 2012 (before then, all income tax counts as personal income tax). The regional share of personal income tax is the communities' own tax; the shares of VAT and excise duties they receive under the financing system count as transfers from the State, as do the system's funds. Navarra collects its taxes itself. In the País Vasco the three provincial councils (Diputaciones Forales) collect them, and the accounts count those councils as local government, so the Basque government's revenue arrives as transfers from them. A community's % of GDP uses its own GDP (from the IGAE's figures as a share of it); per person, its population (Eurostat, the mean of 1 January of the year and the next). The communities together are set against Spain's GDP and population.

The State's budget. The plan by policy comes from the Ministry of Finance's budget statistics (the Secretaría de Estado de Presupuestos y Gastos), which give each year's initial appropriations consolidated across the State, its agencies and the Social Security; a rolled-over budget repeats the previous one's appropriations, so pensions, for one, appear unchanged although they are revalued every year. What the State has spent comes from the IGAE's monthly execution of the State budget, which the IGAE publishes in Excel from 2025; the series grows from there.

Measures. Constant prices use the GDP deflator ({{ bx.price_year }} = 100), the price index of everything the economy produces; per person, Eurostat's average population; % of GDP, nominal GDP for the year.

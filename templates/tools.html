Economy vs pre-COVID trend

Where output, jobs and prices stand against the path they were on before the pandemic. The trend is fitted to the years you choose, up to the last reading before COVID, and extended to today.

{{ figure("trend-line", "Actual and pre-COVID trend", "Index, last pre-COVID reading of the trend = 100", "INE, Seguridad Social; EconPulse calculation") }} {{ figure("trend-gaps", "Gap with the pre-COVID trend", "Every series, same trend years; % above or below", "INE, Seguridad Social; EconPulse calculation") }}

The trend is a straight line through the logarithm of each series (constant growth), fitted from January of the start year to December 2019 (the fourth quarter for quarterly series). Registrations and consumer prices are seasonally adjusted by EconPulse; the others are adjusted by INE. A gap shows how far the economy is from where the old trend pointed, not where it should be: the start year matters, which is why you can change it.

Taylor rule

The policy rate a simple rule suggests, given inflation and slack. Change the assumptions and compare the result with what the ECB actually did, for the euro area as a whole and for Spain on its own.

{{ figure("taylor-tool", "The ECB rate and the rule", "%, monthly", "Eurostat, ECB; EconPulse calculation") }}

Rule: neutral real rate + inflation + (weight on inflation) × (inflation − 2%) − (weight on unemployment) × (unemployment − its trend). Taylor's 1993 rule is 0.5 on inflation and, with an output gap of twice the unemployment gap, 1.0 on unemployment. Trend unemployment is a statistical trend (Hodrick–Prescott filter), least reliable for the latest months. The same assumptions are used on the Analysis page.

Interest rate decomposer

Pick two dates and see what moved Spain's 10-year yield: euro-wide rates or Spain-specific risk, and, within euro-wide rates, near-term policy or the longer run.

Part of the moveChange
{{ figure("rates-parts", "The move against the AAA curve", "Basis points", "Banco de España, ECB; EconPulse calculation") }} {{ figure("rates-levels", "10-year yields over the period", "%, daily", "Banco de España, ECB") }}

Pure arithmetic, no model: Spain's yield is the German yield plus the prima de riesgo, and also the AAA euro-area 10-year yield plus Spain's spread over it. The AAA 10-year is one tenth of today's 1-year rate plus nine tenths of the rate implied for years 1 to 10 (the forward rate); the first moves with expected ECB policy, the second with the longer-run outlook and the term premium. AAA yields are the ECB's curve estimate; dates without data use the last day before.
